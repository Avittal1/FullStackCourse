Life in Slavery in Egypt

The story of the Exodus from Egypt begins thousands of years ago in the ancient Near East. The Children of Israel originally arrived in the fertile Delta region of Egypt as a small extended family, seeking refuge during a time of severe famine in the land of Canaan. At first, the Egyptian authorities welcomed them with open arms due to the high stature of Joseph in the royal court.

However, as generations passed, the population of the Children of Israel grew exponentially into a large and powerful community. A new Pharaoh ascended the throne who did not remember Joseph's historic contributions. He began to fear the sheer size of the Israelite population, worrying that in the event of a foreign invasion or war, they might join forces with Egypt's enemies and fight against the kingdom.

Driven by paranoia and political caution, Pharaoh decided to subjugate the Israelites, reducing them to brutal slavery. For centuries, they endured grueling labor, constructing massive treasure cities such as Pithom and Rameses, working endlessly in agricultural fields under the scorching sun, and suffering under harsh decrees meant to weaken their spirit and numbers.

The Cry and the Promise of Change

The crushing weight of slavery and unrelenting toil created immense suffering throughout the community. In their deepest distress, the Children of Israel cried out for help, yearning for relief from oppression and a fundamental change in their national destiny. According to the ancient biblical account, their groans were heard, setting into motion a miraculous chain of historical events.

A humble shepherd named Moses, who had fled Egypt years earlier and lived in the wilderness of Midian, was chosen to return and confront the powerful regime. Commissioned with a divine directive, Moses returned to Pharaoh's royal court alongside his brother Aaron to demand the immediate release of his people.

"Thus says the Lord: Let my people go, that they may serve me in the wilderness."

The Ten Plagues and the Journey to Freedom

Moses confronted Pharaoh directly, delivering the famous mandate demanding freedom for his people. Yet Pharaoh remained steadfast and hard-hearted, repeatedly refusing to liberate the vast labor force that built his empire's infrastructure. To demonstrate authority and break Pharaoh's resolve, a series of ten devastating plagues struck the land of Egypt—ranging from the Nile turning to blood and swarms of frogs to total darkness and severe natural disasters.

Each plague severely damaged the Egyptian economy and infrastructure, demonstrating the high cost of keeping an entire nation in bondage. Only after the catastrophic tenth plague did Pharaoh finally relent, ordering the Israelites to leave Egypt immediately.

The Children of Israel departed in immense haste—so quickly that their bread dough had no time to rise, obligating them to bake flat matzah on their journey. They set out into the vast Sinai Desert towards the Land of Israel, embarking on a transformative pilgrimage from physical servitude toward national liberty.

Additional Details to Discover:

How long were the Children of Israel in Egypt?

According to ancient tradition and historical calculation, the Children of Israel remained in Egypt for 210 years before gaining their ultimate freedom.

Why do people eat matzah?

Because they left Egypt in great haste, leaving no time for their bread dough to ferment and rise into regular bread.

Where did the Children of Israel march?

They marched through the rugged Sinai Desert for forty years with the goal of reaching the promised Land of Israel.
